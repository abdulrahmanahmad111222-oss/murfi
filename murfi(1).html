    Editorial Policy and Code of Ethics

    Constitutional Rights

    The Nigerian Constitution accords all citizens the right to form and express opinion. This assurance allows citizens to generate, adopt, and impart opinions.

    Section 22 of the Constitution states: "The press, radio, television and other agencies of the mass media will at all times be free to uphold the fundamental objectives contained in this Chapter and uphold the responsibility and accountability of the Government to the people."

    Against this backdrop, the press has the right to research and propagate information freely to citizens, while being protected in the course of this duty. At Murfi, proprietors, editors, and reporters exercise these rights within the boundary of publishing only factual and accurate information that helps citizens reach an informed and responsible judgment on democratic processes and other public matters.

    1. Editorial Independence

    1.1 Murfi is loyal only to the cause that advances the collective interest of the public, including unity, economic prosperity, and security.

    1.2 No individual, group, or organisation may use Murfi to pursue an end that undermines or contradicts the collective interest of the public.

    1.3 Murfi will not be swayed by religious, ethnic, tribal, political, or economic sentiment. We treat all entities equally.

    2. Reporting the News

    2.1 We approach all news fairly, honestly, and factually.

    2.2 News is adequately contextualised and balanced. Facts will not be embellished, distorted, misinterpreted, misrepresented, exaggerated, or sensationalised.

    2.3 All sides of a story, especially critical reporting, are given reasonable notice in advance of publication.

    2.4 Except on rare occasions of strong and overriding public interest, we do not publish information obtained without transparency to the subject of a story.

    2.5 Unless it is absolutely necessary, we de-emphasise the ethnicity, religion, gender, sexual orientation, political affiliation, race, social or economic status, disability, and similar characteristics of the people in our stories.

    3. Investigative Journalism

    3.1 We devote resources to investigative journalism, which checks injustice, corruption, and abuse of power, and promotes transparency and accountability in society.

    4. Fact and Opinion

    4.1 We clearly distinguish between fact and opinion. A factual article must be able to withstand close scrutiny.

    4.2 An opinion article contains some factual elements and is not based on pure fabrication.

    4.3 Satire is labelled as such.

    4.4 Our news journalists keep their articles free of opinion and bias.

    4.5 News analysis contains more facts than viewpoints.

    5. Advocacy

    5.1 We take part in matters of public interest, especially against corruption, police and military brutality, terrorism, and sexual and gender-based violence.

    5.2 All campaigns are based on facts, without distortion or omission.

    5.3 In editorials, we may weigh in on national issues by analysing public policies and offering alternative ideas in a fair, constructive, and honest manner.

    6. Sensitivity

    6.1 Stories, photos, videos, or illustrations involving suicide, death, nudity, mental illness, or obscenity are handled with care and sensitivity.

    6.2 Reporters approach comments and enquiries with sympathy and an open mind.

    7. Children

    7.1 We do not normally interview or film children under 18 without the consent of a parent or guardian.

    7.2 Unless the public interest is clear, children are not interviewed or photographed at schools, parks, or other public facilities without the permission of the people in charge.

    7.3 We do not publish the names, addresses, or other identifying details of children involved in sexual exploitation, trafficking, or abuse, unless a court orders otherwise or it is needed to seek justice.

    8. Victims of Rape, Sexual Assault, and Harassment

    8.1 We do not publish the identity or image of victims of rape or other sexual or gender-based violence, unless they consent.

    8.2 Convicted rapists and perpetrators of sexual or gender-based violence receive no such protection from us.

    8.3 Editorial staff receive regular training on gender issues and gender-sensitive reporting.

    8.4 We build gender awareness into our story writing and our partnerships.

    9. Privacy and the Public Interest

    9.1 We show care and respect for the privacy of all people, except where a strong public interest requires otherwise.

    9.2 A story is considered to be in the public interest where it exposes violent crime or corrupt practices, especially in public service.

    9.3 Issues affecting public health and wellbeing are also in the public interest and can take precedence over the privacy of an individual who poses a threat to them.

    10. News Gathering and Anonymous Sources

    10.1 Our journalists must not gather news through intimidation or duress.

    10.2 Unless the matter is in the public interest, our journalists do not film people on private property without their knowledge and permission.

    10.3 Journalists must not harass sources by phone, email, social media, or repeated contact with people who have said they do not want to be interviewed.

    10.4 We openly identify sources wherever possible, because this strengthens the credibility of a story.

    10.5 Sometimes the only way to get the facts is from people who cannot be named, for fear of backlash or a wish to stay out of the spotlight. Editors decide these cases individually.

    10.6 Where an editor agrees to protect a source's anonymity, the journalist must keep that source's identity confidential permanently.

    10.7 For further fact-finding, an editor may ask a reporter for the identity of an anonymous source. The source is told promptly why this is needed and is assured of confidentiality.

    11. Freelance Contributors

    11.1 Our journalists may not report or supply news material to organisations that compete with Murfi.

    11.2 If a local or international outlet wants to use one of our journalists, the journalist first discusses it with an editor or supervisor.

    11.3 We aim for an equitable gender balance when hiring freelance journalists, opinion writers, and digital content creators.

    12. Bribes and Gifts

    12.1 Our journalists must not accept bribes ("brown envelopes") from news sources or organisations to write a story or receive a mention.

    12.2 Journalists are strongly encouraged to refuse gifts from newsmakers. If refusing a gift would be culturally inappropriate, the journalist must declare it to an editor or senior editorial staff member.

    12.3 Murfi does not demand or accept payment for news coverage.

    12.4 Journalists may accept gifts worth up to ₦10,000. Cash gifts are never accepted.

    12.5 Journalists may take part in lotteries, promotions, or discount programmes open to the general public.

    13. Errors and Corrections

    13.1 We retract any news story, opinion, or advertorial found to be dangerous or false.

    13.2 Once we admit an error, we publish an explanation or apology in a prominent place on the website.

    13.3 People who are the subject of erroneous or unfairly harmful articles are given a right of reply, as decided by the editor.

    14. Social Media

    14.1 We encourage our journalists to maintain a social media presence, and to make it prominent if they are comfortable doing so.

    14.2 Reporters may use social media to share their stories and those of colleagues, engage readers, and build relationships with sources.

    14.3 Reporters should avoid partisan and combative discussions online.

    14.4 We encourage journalists to express themselves freely, and trust them to use social media responsibly and in a way that protects Murfi's reputation.

    14.5 Journalists must not make derogatory remarks or stereotypes about people based on ethnicity, religion, or gender.

    15. Other Media Organisations

    15.1 Murfi does not engage in confrontation with other media organisations.

    15.2 We credit the original sources of news material and multimedia, and respect intellectual property rights. Staff may not reuse facts or material from another publication unless this is clearly disclosed.

    15.3 Plagiarism is not excused in any form. It results in strict disciplinary action.

    15.4 We do not prioritise publishing denials of a story that appeared in a rival publication, unless our reporters have established that the story was false and the outlet refuses to add a correction or retraction.

    16. Copyright

    16.1 Murfi owns all intellectual property rights to content submitted by its journalists or commissioned contributors and published in any format.

    17. Digital Advertising and Sponsored Content

    17.1 Quality journalism costs money. We accept business promotions, banners, and other commercial content to sustain our work.

    17.2 Commercial material is carefully checked against our editorial standards before publication.

    17.3 We may publish sponsored content that is not normally newsworthy, if our editors judge it useful to readers and unlikely to cause harm.

    17.4 Sponsored content is clearly marked. A note appears at the top or bottom of any article or multimedia item that comes from our advertising department, so readers always know what they are seeing.

    18. Multimedia

    18.1 We publish photos and videos taken by our reporters or sourced from social media and elsewhere online. Staff must not manipulate such material in a misleading way.

    18.2 Only simple enhancements are applied to video or images. Any material altered for clarity is labelled as altered.

    19. Viewpoints

    19.1 We publish opinions, analysis, and features that are as diverse and inclusive as possible. We ask contributors not to submit articles that express discrimination or hostility based on sex, ethnicity, religion, race, sexual orientation, disability, or social or economic status.

    19.2 Commentaries are the sole responsibility of their authors.

    20. Administrative Action

    The Managing Editor may set up a panel of inquiry into alleged breaches of these editorial guidelines by members of staff.
